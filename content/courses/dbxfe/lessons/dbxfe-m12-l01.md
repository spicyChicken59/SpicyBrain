<!-- section:why -->

Your calendar contains a demo rehearsal, an unanswered security question, and a request to polish slides. Decide what most improves the customer's next decision without inventing an internal performance scorecard.

<!-- section:understand -->

Prioritization is a judgment about which action changes the next decision most. Consider impact, uncertainty, urgency, dependencies, and effort. A small task that removes a blocking assumption can be more useful than a large task that improves an already-understood part of the story.

For fictional Cinderline, confirming a permitted source path may matter more than adding another demo chart. If access is impossible under the current constraints, the pilot design changes. If access is feasible, the team can proceed with a narrower uncertainty. Make that dependency visible to the account team.

Use an explicit next action: one owner, a concrete output, and an agreed date or condition. “Follow up” is too vague. “Ask the security lead to confirm classification and permitted path before finalizing the pilot charter” says what is needed and why. This is SpicyBrain working advice, not a Databricks quota, internal process, or required ramp schedule.

**Fictional week triage.**

| Agree the quality denominator | Determines correctness evidence | Next, with quality/operations |

The sequence is conditional. A changed meeting date or new blocker can change the order.

<!-- section:deeper -->

Protect time for learning connected to actual work. After a shadowed call, identify one concept you could not explain and use the relevant lesson. Ask a teammate to challenge your explanation. Avoid equating visible busyness with customer progress. If competing requests cannot all fit, share the decision tradeoff and ask the appropriate owner to prioritize rather than silently missing commitments.

<!-- section:customer -->

For the account team: “The source-path question can change the pilot design, so I propose resolving it before expanding the demo. In parallel, we need the quality owner to accept the denominator. Then the rehearsal can reflect an agreed and feasible story.”

<!-- section:try -->

Rank three fictional tasks: make a new platform overview deck, resolve an unverified mandatory security requirement, and capture the current quality baseline. Explain the dependencies and who should confirm the priority.

<!-- section:revisit -->

Resolve the mandatory security requirement first or establish its review path, because it can block execution. Collect the quality baseline as the next decision-enabling task, potentially in parallel if it uses already approved data and has its own owner. The overview deck is lower priority unless a specific audience decision depends on it. Confirm the tradeoff with the account/customer owners rather than assuming your ranking is an internal rule.

The point is explicit reasoning: what decision changes, what dependency is removed, and what evidence becomes available?
