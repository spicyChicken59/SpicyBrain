<!-- section:why -->

A sponsor asks whether the quality project pays for itself. You can create a useful illustrative model without pretending that hypothetical savings or vendor rates are observed facts.

<!-- section:understand -->

Separate the cost model from the benefit model. Cost depends on usage, applicable rates, implementation, operations, storage/networking, and other relevant charges. Benefit depends on a change in work that the customer can actually realize. Avoid double-counting the same hour as both labor savings and another financial benefit.

All numbers in this lesson are hypothetical. Suppose a reporting improvement saves 10 hours per week for 48 weeks, valued at $60 per hour. The annual gross labor-value hypothesis is 10 × 48 × 60 = $28,800. Suppose incremental recurring cost is $12,000 per year and one-time implementation is $15,000. Year-one net illustrative value is $1,800; recurring-year net is $16,800 before omitted costs.

Released staff time is not automatically a cash saving. The customer may redeploy time rather than reduce spending. The model is a conversation about drivers and uncertainty, not a guarantee or a Databricks quotation.

<!-- section:see -->

**Hypothetical sensitivity table; all rates and costs are invented teaching inputs.**

| Hours saved / week | Gross annual labor value | Year-one net after $27,000 assumed cost |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | $14,400 | -$12,600 |
| 10 | $28,800 | $1,800 |
| 15 | $43,200 | $16,200 |

Break-even hours are $27,000 ÷ (48 × $60) = 9.375 hours/week. The diagram shows that hours saved and actual realization drive the benefit, while recurring and transition costs drive the other side.

<!-- section:deeper -->

Before using real rates, verify product, cloud, region, edition, workload, commitment/discount terms, and excluded charges from current authoritative sources. This course deliberately uses no real price inputs. Add sensitivity for usage growth, migration delay, dual operation, and support effort. State the evaluation period and whether one-time costs are included. If uncertain inputs change the decision, prioritize measuring them during the pilot.

<!-- section:customer -->

For the sponsor: “This is an illustrative model, not a savings promise. At the stated inputs, year one is only slightly positive and becomes negative if the time benefit is lower. We should measure realistic time released and complete the cost basis before using it for an investment decision.”

<!-- section:try -->

Using the hypothetical inputs, calculate year-one net value if only 6 hours per week are saved. Then name three omitted items that could change the decision and explain why released time may not become cash savings.

<!-- section:revisit -->

Gross value is 6 × 48 × $60 = $17,280. Subtract $12,000 recurring and $15,000 implementation cost: year-one net is -$9,720. Possible omitted items include internal support effort, storage/network charges, training, migration delay, and temporary dual operation. Choose those relevant to the actual proposal.

Released time can improve capacity without reducing payroll. Label it as capacity or labor-value potential unless the customer can explain how it becomes a financial saving. The arithmetic is deterministic; the assumptions still need evidence.
