### Who reports this and how

Databricks publishes this vendor case study about National Australia Bank. The body was inspected on 24 September 2026, including Retiring a heritage platform, Modernizing analytics, and From resilience to innovation. Its publication date is not stated in the retrieved body. Outcomes remain attributed vendor claims.

### The problem

A decades-old Teradata estate supported thousands of feeds and over 140 reporting suites. The vendor describes delays, incidents and embedded dependencies that complicated retirement.

### Constraints

Regulatory continuity, lineage and user enablement accompanied technical migration.

### Architecture as described

The page names Databricks SQL, Auto Loader, declarative pipelines, Unity Catalog and Power BI, with medallion data flows and deployment bundles.

### Evidence and its limits

The vendor page itself reports 95% of critical workloads migrated and more than 1,200 users enabled. This replaces reliance on a search-only social-post title. The denominator and weighting of “critical workloads” remain unspecified. The page describes decommissioning as achieved, but provides no independently reproduced parity or financial audit.

### What transfers

Authored analysis: make the retirement inventory an acceptance artifact, with owners and dependencies. Separate platform adoption, user activity and old-system shutdown. Require report-level parity evidence before removing a regulated reporting path.

### Missing information

Request the definition of critical workloads, the remaining workload disposition, cost and incident-rate baselines, the parallel-run evidence and regulatory sign-off. Do not expand the vendor's 95% claim to all workloads.
